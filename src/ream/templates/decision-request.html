Where should the key for the off-site backup live?

The off-site copy finishes uploading on Thursday and I would rather encrypt it before it is complete than re-upload it afterwards.

Waiting on youAsked

What I already know
  • The off-site provider can read anything we do not encrypt ourselves. Their own at-rest encryption is theirs, not ours — it protects the disk, not us.
  • We already back the vault machine up separately, so a key held only there is not a single point of failure in the way it first looks.
  • I checked whether the provider supports customer-supplied keys. They do, but it does not change this choice — the question is still who can be compelled to hand the key over.

The options

a Keep it in the house, on the vault machine onlymy recommendation

The off-site copy is useless to anyone who steals it or is handed it, including the provider.

Costs: if the house burns down with the vault machine in it, the off-site copy is unreadable. Mitigated by the vault's own backup, which is why I lean this way.

b Split it — half here, half with the provider

Survives losing the house, because the provider holds a real half.

Costs: the provider can be compelled to produce their half, and a determined attacker needs one break-in rather than two. Also the most moving parts to get wrong at 3am.

c Give it to the provider

Simplest to restore from, and restoring is the thing that actually has to work.

Costs: it is encryption in name. Anyone who can reach the provider can read the backup.
