Where the off-site backups should go

Three ways to hold a copy of the house outside the house, judged on what happens when we actually need it.

Recommending the object storeWritten

Recommendation: a plain object store at a provider we do not otherwise use.

It is the only one of the three where the thing that fails in the house cannot also take out the copy. The other two are cheaper, and one is much faster to restore from, but both share something with the thing they are protecting — and sharing is the whole risk.

What I judged them on

  1. Independence — can one event destroy both the original and the copy? This outranks everything else, because it is the only failure we could not come back from.
  2. Restore time — how long from "we need it" to "we have it".
  3. Cost — per month, at about 2 TB, including what it costs to get the data back OUT, which is the number providers are quietest about.
  4. How much it can break — parts that need looking after.

The three

A plain object store, elsewhererecommended

Completely independent: different company, different building, different failure modes from anything here. Nothing to maintain. Restores are a download.

Slowest to restore — about nine hours for the lot over this line — and the egress charge means a full restore costs real money at exactly the moment nobody is thinking about money.

A second machine, at a friend's house

Cheapest to run and much faster to restore from, because we can drive over and collect the disk. Full control of the format.

It is a machine, so it is ours to look after, remotely, in someone else's house. Independence is good but not total: the same bad update or the same mistake by us can reach both ends.

A disk in a drawer, swapped monthly

Utterly independent — it is not plugged into anything. Nothing to pay and nothing to break. Restores at USB speed.

Only as current as the last swap, so it loses up to a month. And it depends on a human remembering, monthly, forever, which is the part that has never once worked here.

What would change my mind

  • If a full restore turned out to be needed more than about once a year, nine hours stops being acceptable and the friend's machine wins.
  • If the egress charge is worse than quoted at 2 TB. I have priced it once, from their published rates, and not tested it.
